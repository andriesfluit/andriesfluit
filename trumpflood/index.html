Methodology

How the zone is decided, where the data comes from, and what to be skeptical about.

Daily collection

Three times a day — 06:00, 12:00 and 18:00 UTC, which is 08:00 / 14:00 / 20:00 Belgian local in summer (CEST) and 07:00 / 13:00 / 19:00 in winter (CET) — a script fetches headlines from 31 Belgian RSS feeds: Google News BE (Dutch & French general), 8 Google News BE topic feeds (politics, world, business, tech, sport in NL; politics, world, business in FR), and 21 direct outlet feeds spanning VRT NWS, HLN, De Standaard, De Morgen, Het Nieuwsblad, GVA, HBVL, Knack, Sporza, Bruzz on the Dutch side, plus RTBF, La Libre, L'Echo, DH, 7sur7, BX1 directly, and De Tijd, Le Soir, Sudinfo, L'Avenir and RTL through Google News' site: filter (their direct feeds sit behind Cloudflare). De Standaard's direct feed is reached through the cloudscraper library to handle the basic Cloudflare JS challenge. Articles whose pubDate is today (Belgian local date) are kept; duplicates across feeds are removed by URL.

Core vs. wide tier

Not every feed is equal. Brussels-only outlets (BX1, Bruzz), sport-only outlets (Sporza), and the Google News aggregator feeds (which repackage content we already pull directly) structurally dilute the denominator with stories that are local, single-subject, or duplicated. To avoid that skew, the headline number on this page uses a core tier: national and regional-generalist outlets only — VRT, RTBF, De Standaard, De Morgen, HLN, Het Nieuwsblad, GVA, HBVL, Knack, La Libre, L'Echo, DHnet, 7sur7, plus De Tijd, Le Soir, Sudinfo, L'Avenir and RTL (reached via Google News' site: filter because their direct RSS is Cloudflare-gated). The full wide corpus is still computed and stored in data/log.json as a cross-check. Today’s core corpus: 609 headlines, 16 by-name Trump matches = 2.6% (this is the share the zone uses; see the “Trump match” section below for how indirect references are handled separately).

Including every feed we fetch — Brussels-local (BX1, Bruzz), sport-only (Sporza), and the Google News aggregators that repackage outlets we already pull directly — the full wide corpus is 816 headlines with 24 Trump matches = 2.9% (+0.3pt vs. core). The cross-check exists so we can spot a day where the two tiers diverge sharply, which usually signals an aggregator artefact rather than a real shift. The 7-day rolling average of the core share is 2.25%.

Trump match

Each headline title is scanned with two detectors at once:

  • Name-only (drives the zone). A single case-insensitive match on trump (the literal name, whole word). Every number that feeds the zone classifier — share, breadth, rank, dominance — uses this detector, so Trump is measured on exactly the same yardstick as the other named figures we track.
  • Expanded (shown, but does not drive the zone). A broader detector that also catches common indirect references: white house / witte huis / maison blanche, oval office / bureau ovale, and US president / Amerikaans(e) president / président(e) américain(e) / président(e) des États-Unis / president van de VS (accents optional). A headline like “Het Witte Huis waarschuwt Europa” is counted here. This total is shown as editorial context, but it does not feed the zone.

Why split them? Expanding Trump’s pattern while the other figures stay name-only would inflate Trump’s share against comparators who only match by name. Keeping both numbers separate is the honest version: the zone is computed apples-to-apples, and the expanded figure is published so you can see how much extra coverage the indirect references add on any given day. Titles only; no body text, no fuzzy matching.

Comparators & themes

The same headlines are scanned for fourteen other named people (Trump is the fifteenth) split into two groups:

  • International (4). Putin, Macron, Netanyahu, Zelensky. Heads of state whose actions recurringly drive Belgian front-page news.
  • Belgian (10). De Wever, Bouchez, Magnette, Prévot, Rousseau, Francken, Crevits, De Croo, Van Peteghem, Verlinden. Sitting federal PM, party presidents of the main federal-coalition and opposition parties, and ministers who are recurrently named in Belgian headlines.

Admission rule. The list is reviewed manually each quarter. A figure is in the list if they (a) hold a current senior political office in Belgium (federal PM, federal minister, major party president) or (b) lead a country whose actions make recurring Belgian front-page news. Removed in the most recent review: Orbán, Meloni and Musk (intermittent day-to-day Belgian salience); Jambon (the surname collides with the French word for ham). Once the live archive is 90+ days, this editorial rule can be replaced by “anyone with ≥ N name mentions in the trailing 90-day core corpus”, which would make the list self-maintaining.

The same headlines are also scanned for fourteen broad subject categories (war, crime, EU politics, Belgian government, etc.) using multi-language regex (NL + FR + EN keywords) so “klimaat” and “climat” both count as Climate.

Zone assessment

No single number can honestly say “Trump is flooding Belgian news today”. The zone combines four signals, each answering a different question. Every zone above Dry requires several of them to clear a floor simultaneously — a single-signal spike is never enough.

1. Share — “How much of the news is about Trump?”

The percentage of today’s Belgian core-tier headlines that mention Trump by name. Computed as name-only Trump headlines ÷ total headlines × 100. The expanded number (name + indirect references) is shown separately in the hero, but the zone is driven by this name-only figure to keep Trump and the fourteen comparators on one yardstick.

Today: 16/609 = 2.6%. A low number means Trump simply is not in the news much by name, regardless of what other signals say.

2. Dominance — “Is he THE figure of the day?”

Trump’s name-only mentions divided by the sum of mentions of the other fourteen named figures we track (4 international + 10 Belgian; see the Comparators list). A value above 1.0× means Trump alone out-mentions the other fourteen combined.

Today: 3.2×. This catches the “slow domestic day” case where Trump scoops up all named-figure attention even when coverage volume is modest.

3. Breadth — “Is it everywhere, or one paper?”

The fraction of core outlets that published at least one Trump headline today. Only outlets with ≥ 5 headlines count toward the denominator so a near-empty feed doesn’t skew the ratio.

Today: 65% of core outlets ran a Trump story. A high dominance combined with low breadth means one outlet is obsessing; a high breadth means Belgian newsrooms collectively decided Trump deserves a spot today.

4. Rank — “Is he on top at all?”

Trump’s position among the fifteen named figures, by mention count. Acts as a veto: higher zones require Trump to be #1 or #2. If he’s #5 on a day everyone is talking about Macron, no zone upgrade follows.

Today: rank #1 of 15. For context, Trump would rank #4 of 15 against broad subject themes (war, crime, EU politics, ...) — but themes bundle dozens of stories so a single person rarely beats them. Theme rank is context, never a zone driver.

Deviation (context, not a hard gate)

Today’s core share divided by the 14-day median. Requires at least 7 prior live-day records, which we only build up gradually. Until then this signal is treated as “pass”.

Today: — (not enough live history yet). The 7-day rolling average of the core share is 2.25%.

The thresholds are calibrated against our core-tier RSS corpus, which is narrower than GDELT’s full Belgian feed. In practice that means 2.5% of our core headlines being about Trump is a meaningful signal of dominance; the same percentage in GDELT’s corpus would be routine. Once we have 30+ days of live history, these absolute thresholds can be replaced by percentiles of Trump’s own history (“Flooding = top-10% day ever recorded”), which is the only truly self-calibrated version.

Caveats & limits

  • Title-only matching. Titles are scanned, not article bodies. Indirect references that the expanded detector does not cover (“the administration”, “Washington”, “POTUS”) are still missed. Smaller undercount than before the NL / FR / EN expansion, but non-zero.
  • Trump-the-family. The regex also matches “Trump Jr.”, “Eric Trump”, “Trump Tower”. Usually ≤2% noise but it’s there.
  • Time-of-day sampling & peak-of-day rule. Fetches run three times a day at fixed UTC slots (06:00 / 12:00 / 18:00), which shifts by an hour between CEST and CET. RSS feeds only expose the latest N items, so an afternoon fetch may show fewer Trump headlines than the morning one because earlier pieces have scrolled off. To avoid understating a Trump-heavy day, we keep the peak observation for each day: if a later run sees a lower share than the one already stored, we keep the earlier record and only update the “last checked” timestamp. Later runs still replace the record when they see a higher share, so a late-breaking Trump surge is captured.
  • Dedup policy. URL duplicates (same article reached via two feeds, e.g. Google News republishing an HLN link) collapse to one globally. Title duplicates only collapse within the same outlet — a normalised title (lower-case, accent-stripped, editorial prefixes like “VIDEO.” removed, first 80 chars compared) that repeats inside one publisher’s feed becomes one. Titles shorter than 12 characters after normalisation are not deduped (generic short strings like “update” can collide on unrelated stories). But if HLN, Nieuwsblad and GVA all run the same Reuters headline, those are three editorial decisions and all three count. That is what “breadth” measures: publishing choices, not unique authored stories.
  • Aggregator overlap. The Google News feeds (only used in the wide tier, not core) republish outlets we already pull directly; URLs differ from the direct ones so some duplication leaks in on the wide number.
  • Belgian press covers world. The denominator is heavy with international news that Belgian outlets choose to publish. This measures “share of the news Belgians read” — not “what Belgian society is discussing internally”.
  • Comparator list maintenance. The fifteen- person comparator set is reviewed editorially each quarter under the admission rule above. Between reviews, a newly-prominent Belgian figure who is not on the list inflates Trump’s rank until they are added. Once the live archive reaches 90+ days, the list can be replaced by a data-driven rule (“anyone with ≥ N name mentions in the trailing 90 days”) and the manual review falls away.
  • Arbitrary absolute thresholds. The 4.0% / 2.5% / 1.5% / 0.8% share cutoffs plus the 2.0× / 1.2× dominance and 55% / 40% / 25% breadth floors are chosen rather than calibrated against historical distributions. As the archive of live days grows, these should be re-expressed as percentiles of Trump’s own history.
  • Theme overlap. A headline can match multiple themes (a Gaza article counts for War and for EU politics if Macron is quoted). Themes are not mutually exclusive.
