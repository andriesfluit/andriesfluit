Methodology

How the zone is decided, where the data comes from, and what to be skeptical about.

The name

The quote in the masthead is Steve Bannon’s — Trump’s former chief strategist — describing a media strategy to author Michael Lewis in 2018: overwhelm the press with so much material at once that scrutiny of any single story becomes impossible. The vulgarity is his, quoted for the record, not endorsed. This site borrows the metaphor to measure the effect on the Belgian press — is the zone in fact flooded? — and takes no position on intent.

Daily collection

Three times a day, at 03:37, 10:37 and 16:37 UTC (which is 05:37 / 12:37 / 18:37 Belgian local in summer (CEST) and 04:37 / 11:37 / 17:37 in winter (CET)), a script fetches headlines from 33 Belgian RSS feeds: Google News BE (Dutch & French general), 8 Google News BE topic feeds (politics, world, business, tech, sport in NL; politics, world, business in FR), and 23 outlet feeds spanning VRT NWS, HLN, De Morgen, Het Nieuwsblad, HBVL, Knack, Sporza, Bruzz on the Dutch side, plus RTBF, La Libre, L'Echo, DH, 7sur7, BX1 directly, and De Standaard, De Tijd, Le Soir, Sudinfo, L'Avenir, RTL, Trends and Trends-Tendances through Google News’ site: filter or cloudscraper (outlets whose direct RSS feeds are Cloudflare-gated; De Standaard uses cloudscraper to preserve full paywall-headline coverage). Articles whose pubDate is today (Belgian local date) are kept; URL duplicates are collapsed globally, and near-identical titles inside a single outlet’s own feed are collapsed too (full rules under “Dedup policy” in the caveats).

Core vs. wide tier

Not every feed is equal. Brussels-only outlets (BX1, Bruzz), sport-only outlets (Sporza), and the Google News aggregator feeds (which repackage content we already pull directly) structurally dilute the denominator with stories that are local, single-subject, or duplicated. To avoid that skew, the headline number on this page uses a core tier: national and regional-generalist outlets only — VRT, RTBF, De Standaard, De Morgen, HLN, Het Nieuwsblad, HBVL, Knack, La Libre, L'Echo, DHnet, 7sur7, plus De Tijd, Le Soir, Sudinfo, L'Avenir, RTL, Trends and Trends-Tendances (reached via Google News’ site: filter or cloudscraper because their direct RSS is Cloudflare-gated). GVA is fetched but kept in the wide tier only, as its national content largely overlaps with Het Nieuwsblad (same group). The full wide corpus is still computed and stored in data/log.json as a cross-check. Trump match

A headline is a Trump headline if its title matches the regex \btrumps?\b (whole word, case-insensitive; the optional s covers the Dutch genitive “Trumps uitnodiging”, counted since 2026-07-11 — see caveats), unless the only match is a family member or a Trump-branded property (also see caveats). All four zone signals (share, breadth, dominance, rank) run on this single name-only definition, so Trump is measured on the same yardstick as the other sixteen named figures in the comparator list. Titles only; no body text, no fuzzy matching.

As a secondary readout we also count headlines that refer to Trump indirectly, by role or location rather than by name: white house / witte huis / maison blanche, oval office / bureau ovale, and US president / Amerikaans(e) president / président(e) américain(e) / président(e) des États-Unis / president van de VS. This count is reported as “indirect references” in the daily record and does not drive the zone; it only flags days when Belgian quality press is writing about the administration without naming Trump directly.

Comparators

The same headlines are scanned for seventeen named people in total. The set is split 7 international / 10 Belgian:

  • International (7, including Trump). Trump, Putin, Macron, Netanyahu, Zelensky, Rutte (NATO Secretary General), Von der Leyen (EU Commission President). Heads of state, government, or major international institutions whose actions recurringly drive Belgian front-page news. Trump sits inside this group for rank and dominance; he is measured by the same name-only regex as the other six.
  • Belgian (10). De Wever, Bouchez, Magnette, Prévot, Rousseau, Francken, Crevits, Jambon, Van Peteghem, Verlinden. Sitting federal PM, party presidents of the main federal-coalition and opposition parties, and ministers who are recurrently named in Belgian headlines.

Admission rule. The list is reviewed manually each quarter. A figure is in the list if they (a) hold a current senior political office in Belgium (federal PM, federal minister, major party president) or (b) lead a country or major international institution whose actions make recurring Belgian front-page news. Removed in the most recent review: Orbán, Meloni and Musk (intermittent day-to-day Belgian salience); De Croo (left government February 2025). Added: Rutte, Von der Leyen, Jambon. Once the live archive is 90+ days, this editorial rule can be replaced by “anyone with ≥ N name mentions in the trailing 90-day core corpus”, which would make the list self-maintaining.

Fourteen broad subject themes (war, crime, EU politics, Belgian government, etc.) are still counted on every run and stored in data/log.json as background context, but they are no longer shown on the page and no longer compared to Trump. Mixing a single person with aggregate themes is a category error: themes bundle dozens of stories and a person almost never out-ranks an aggregate, which produced visually dramatic but structurally meaningless “Trump beats Sports” lines.

Zone assessment

No single number can honestly say “Trump is flooding Belgian news today”. The zone combines four signals, each answering a different question. Every zone above Dry requires several of them to clear a floor simultaneously — a single-signal spike is never enough.

1. Share — “How much of the news is about Trump?”

The percentage of today’s Belgian core-tier headlines that name Trump. Computed as Trump-by-name headlines ÷ total core headlines × 100.

Today: 68/595 = 11.4%. A low number means Trump is not prominent in the news today, regardless of what other signals say.

2. Dominance — “Is he THE figure of the day?”

Trump’s name-only mentions divided by the sum of mentions of the other sixteen named figures we track (6 international + 10 Belgian; see the Comparators list). A value above 1.0× means Trump alone out-mentions the other sixteen combined.

Today: 3.78×. This catches the “slow domestic day” case where Trump scoops up all named-figure attention even when coverage volume is modest.

3. Breadth — “Is it everywhere, or one paper?”

The fraction of core outlets that published at least one Trump-by-name headline today. Only outlets with ≥ 5 post-dedup headlines count toward the denominator so a near-empty feed doesn’t skew the ratio.

Today: 89% of core outlets ran a Trump story. A high dominance combined with low breadth means one outlet is obsessing; a high breadth means Belgian newsrooms collectively decided Trump deserves a spot today.

4. Rank — “Is he on top at all?”

Trump’s position among the seventeen named figures, by name-only mention count (the same yardstick used for share, dominance and breadth). Acts as a veto: higher zones require Trump to be #1 or #2. Ties favour Trump — if Trump and Macron both hit 5 mentions, Trump is called #1, not #2. If he’s #5 on a day everyone is talking about Macron, no zone upgrade follows.

Today: rank #1 of 17.

Deviation (annotation only)

Today’s name-only core share divided by the 14-day median of the same series. This is not a zone gate. It is reported next to the zone so a reader can see whether today is unusual for Belgium’s own baseline, but it does not decide the classification: an earlier version included deviation as a gate on Flooding and it never actually filtered anything, because any day that cleared the other four Flooding floors always cleared the deviation floor as well. Requires at least 7 prior days of data, which the archive builds up gradually.

Today: 3.93× the 14-day median. The 7-day rolling average of the core share (live RSS days only, GDELT backfill excluded) is 4.73%.

Thresholds in use today: version natural-breakpoints-2026-07-11. “Flooding the zone” is a claim about crowding out everything else, so the zones are defined on the signals that measure exactly that, using only natural breakpoints — values that mean something by definition, not by tuning. Flooding: he is the day’s #1 figure with double the mentions of the sixteen other tracked figures combined (dominance ≥ 2), across a majority of outlets. Soaked: #1 and he alone out-mentions the sixteen others combined (dominance ≥ 1 — parity, a breakpoint given by arithmetic). Wet: a top-2 figure. Puddles: a top-4 figure. One external number remains: a materiality floor on every zone above Dry (share ≥ 1.15%, the median day of a normal presidency — Biden in spring 2022, measured on the same Belgian press via GDELT and converted with a scale factor measured on 83 overlapping days), so a #1 spot on a dead news day cannot count as flooding. Two principles hold throughout: no gate references Trump’s own distribution (calibrating on the anomaly would redefine the abnormal level as the baseline), and no gate was tuned to make any zone rare or common — how often each zone occurs is an empirical outcome, not a design target.

Caveats & limits

  • Title-only matching, name-only for the zone. Titles are scanned, not article bodies. Only the literal name trump drives the zone, so a day of coverage that reaches for “the White House”, “the administration”, “Washington” or “POTUS” without saying his name reads as Dry-leaning even if the expanded detector catches some of it. That tradeoff is deliberate: it keeps Trump on the same yardstick as the other sixteen figures. The expanded count is published next to the headline number so you can see, on any given day, how much coverage an indirect-only readout would have added.
  • Trump-the-family filtered out. The bare trump regex would also match “Trump Jr.”, “Eric Trump”, “Trump Tower”, “Trump Organization” and similar. Each of those patterns (Jr / Junior, Eric / Ivanka / Melania / Barron / Tiffany / Lara, Tower / Hotel / Plaza / Golf / International / Organization) is stripped from the headline before re-checking for trump, so a building or relative on its own does not drive the count. Mixed headlines like “Donald Trump met Trump Jr.” still count once: stripping “Trump Jr.” leaves “Donald Trump” for the re-match.
  • Dutch genitive counted since 2026-07-11. Dutch writes the possessive of consonant-ending names without an apostrophe (“Trumps uitnodiging”), which the original trump missed (≈0.4% of archived Trump headlines). All consonant-ending comparator names now accept a trailing s — applied to every figure at once (Poetins, De Wevers, ...) so rank and dominance stay symmetric. Daily records carry a pattern_version marker, so days before and after the change are distinguishable in the archive.
  • Time-of-day sampling & peak-of-day rule. Fetches run three times a day at fixed UTC slots (03:37 / 10:37 / 16:37), which shifts by an hour between CEST and CET. RSS feeds only expose the latest N items, so an afternoon fetch may show fewer Trump headlines than the morning one because earlier pieces have scrolled off. To avoid understating a Trump-heavy day, we keep the peak name-only share for each day: if a later run sees a lower name-only share than the one already stored, we keep the earlier record and only update the “last checked” timestamp. Later runs still replace the record when they see a higher name-only share, so a late-breaking Trump surge is captured.
  • Dedup policy. URL duplicates (same article reached via two feeds, e.g. Google News republishing an HLN link) collapse to one globally. Title duplicates only collapse within the same outlet — a normalised title (lower-case, accent-stripped, editorial prefixes like “VIDEO.” removed, first 80 chars compared) that repeats inside one publisher’s feed becomes one. Titles shorter than 12 characters after normalisation are not deduped (generic short strings like “update” can collide on unrelated stories). But if HLN, Nieuwsblad and GVA all run the same Reuters headline, those are three editorial decisions and all three count. That is what “breadth” measures: publishing choices, not unique authored stories.
  • Aggregator overlap. The Google News feeds (only used in the wide tier, not core) republish outlets we already pull directly; URLs differ from the direct ones so some duplication leaks in on the wide number.
  • Belgian press covers world. The denominator is heavy with international news that Belgian outlets choose to publish. This measures “share of the news Belgians read” — not “what Belgian society is discussing internally”.
  • Comparator list maintenance. The seventeen- person comparator set is reviewed editorially each quarter under the admission rule above. Between reviews, a newly-prominent Belgian figure who is not on the list inflates Trump’s rank until they are added. Once the live archive reaches 90+ days, the list can be replaced by a data-driven rule (“anyone with ≥ N name mentions in the trailing 90 days”) and the manual review falls away.
